## 🩸 Menstrual Blood – High Yield Guide

### 📏 **Estimated Blood Loss**
| Item | Typical Volume |
|-----|----------------:|
| Average per cycle | ~60 mL |
| Period underwear | < 5 mL |
| Regular pads / tampons | 5–10 mL |
| Menstrual discs / cups | Up to 50 mL |

---

### 🎨 **Colour & Clinical Significance**

| Colour | Typical Appearance | When It Occurs | Significance / Considerations |
|:-----:|--------------------|----------------|-----------------------------:|
| 🟤 **Dark brown / black** | Older blood, oxidised | Often at **start or end** of period | Usually normal unless foul odor or other symptoms |
| 🔴 **Bright red** | Fresh blood | **Heaviest flow days** | Active shedding; generally normal |
| 🟥 **Dark red / burgundy** | Slightly older blood, pooled | After lying down / sleeping | Normal; caution if **large clots** or **severe pain** |
| 🩷 **Pink** | Blood mixed with mucus/discharge | Start/end of period; spotting; ovulation | Usually benign; persistent pink may suggest **low estrogen** |
| 🟧 **Orange** | Blood mixed with cervical fluids | Any time | May indicate **infection** if odor or discomfort |
| ⚪ **Gray / off-white** | Unusual discharge colour | Any time | **Abnormal**; may suggest **bacterial vaginosis** or **miscarriage**; requires medical review |

---

### 🩺 **Clinical Context**
- Variations can be normal, especially if consistent cycle to cycle.
- Persistent changes, large clots, severe pain, or foul odor warrant medical evaluation.
- **Royal Children's Hospital**: assess menstrual history and blood characteristics to identify coagulopathies or infections.  
- **ACOG**: abnormal bleeding patterns or unusual colours may be linked to endocrinopathies or serious pathology.

> ✅ While normal variation exists, **persistent or symptomatic changes should always be evaluated**.
