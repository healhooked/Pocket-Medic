## **Stresses of Flight in Aeromedical Retrieval**

When flying, both patients and crew are exposed to environmental and physiological stressors that can impact safety, comfort, and clinical outcomes. These stressors vary by **aircraft type** (fixed-wing vs. rotary) and require careful consideration during planning and patient care.

---

### **1. Acceleration & Deceleration (G Forces)**

- **General info:** Forces are usually minimal during take-off and landing.
- **Orientation matters:**
    - Seated: anteroposterior (front-to-back) forces.
    - Supine (stretcher): along the **long axis** (head-to-toe or toe-to-head).
- **Clinical implications:**
    - Shifts of **organs and fluid volumes** along the body axis.
    - Crash restraint orientation adds complexity – patient positioning should consider G forces.
### **2. Vibration**

- **Sources:**
    
    - Fixed-wing: engine + turbulence.
        
    - Helicopter: main rotor, tail rotor, gearbox, engine.
        
- **Physiological effects:**
    
    - Motion sickness, blurred vision, SOB, chest/abdominal pain (low-frequency).
        
    - Increased metabolic rate, tachycardia, increased BP, peripheral vasoconstriction.
        
- **High-risk groups:**
    
    - Patients with **evolved gas disorders** (e.g., post-diving), neonates, trauma patients.
        

---

### **3. Noise**

- **Sources:** Engines, rotor blades, radios, monitoring equipment.
    
- **Impacts:**
    
    - Communication difficult, stethoscopes ineffective, alarms inaudible.
        
    - Can cause **fatigue, headaches, visual disturbances**, and potential hearing loss.
        
- **Recommendation:** **Hearing protection** for crew and patients.
    

---

### **4. Temperature**

- **Patient risks:** Many have impaired **thermoregulation** due to sepsis, trauma, or anesthesia.
    
- **Environmental factor:** Temperature decreases with altitude.
    
- **Special populations:** Neonates, children, and critically ill patients are especially vulnerable.
    
- **Clinical note:** Hypothermia can worsen **mortality and morbidity** – temperature control is essential.
    

---

### **5. Spatial Disorientation / Vestibular Dysfunction**

- **Visual cues:** Cabin floor usually acts as horizontal reference.
    
- **False horizons:** Tilted aircraft can create disorientation; pilots may unintentionally bank or turn.
    
- **Other cues:**
    
    - Proprioception (body position sensing) is altered in flight.
        
    - Vestibular system senses **linear and rotational acceleration**, but can be fooled, leading to illusions.
        
- **Clinical relevance:** Awareness of disorientation is critical for both **crew safety** and **patient monitoring**.
    

---

### **6. Humidity**

- **Environmental effect:**
    
    - Higher altitudes → lower temperature → lower humidity → **dry cabin air**.
        
- **Clinical implications:**
    
    - Can cause **dehydration** in both patients and crew.
        
    - Important to monitor **fluid status** and provide adequate hydration during long transports.
        

---

### **Summary Table**

|Stressor|Source|Effects|Clinical Considerations|
|---|---|---|---|
|**G Forces**|Take-off/landing|Organ/fluid shifts|Patient positioning, restraint orientation|
|**Vibration**|Engine, rotors, turbulence|Motion sickness, tachycardia, BP ↑|High-risk patients (neonates, trauma, evolved gas disorders)|
|**Noise**|Aircraft systems|Communication issues, fatigue, hearing loss|Hearing protection, adapt monitoring methods|
|**Temperature**|Ambient altitude ↓|Hypothermia, thermoregulation issues|Careful temp management, especially in children/neonates|
|**Spatial Disorientation**|Visual, proprioceptive, vestibular inputs|Illusions, disorientation|Crew awareness, patient monitoring|
|**Humidity**|Altitude|Dehydration|Hydration strategies for crew/patients|

---

### **Key Takeaways for Aeromedical Care**

1. **Patient orientation matters** – G forces can move organs and fluids.
    
2. **Minimise vibration exposure** where possible, especially for vulnerable patients.
    
3. **Protect hearing** and account for communication limitations.
    
4. **Maintain temperature and hydration** – critical for outcomes.
    
5. **Understand spatial disorientation** – for crew safety and situational awareness.
