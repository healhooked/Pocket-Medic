**Module 2: Altitude Physiology**

- Focuses on applied flight physiology and the transport environment.
    
- Covers:
    
    - Gas laws and physiological stresses of flight.
        
    - Clinical implications for patients during aeromedical retrieval.
        
    - Management strategies: chest decompression, cabin pressure, hypoxia, and patient monitoring.
        
    - Effects of altitude on medical equipment (e.g., ET tube cuffs, splints, vacuum mattresses).
        
    - Environmental factors affecting retrieval teams: vibration, acceleration, noise, temperature, and space.
        

**Case Studies:**

- Four scenarios (decompression illness, small bowel obstruction, COPD with hypoxia, penetrating eye and chest injury) used to apply theoretical knowledge to practical aeromedical planning.
    
- Considerations include pathophysiology, relevant gas laws, altitude-related complications, and strategies to mitigate risks.
    
- Team member fitness to fly (recent dental work) also discussed.
    

**Key Concepts:**

- **GHOSTBAN** acronym outlines major stresses of flight:
    
    - G: Gravitational forces
        
    - H: Humidity
        
    - O: Oxygen (hypoxia)
        
    - S: Space (limited cabin)
        
    - T: Temperature variations
        
    - B: Barometric pressure (affects gas volumes)
        
    - A: Acceleration
        
    - N: Noise
        

**Learning Outcomes:**

- Understand how altitude and flight environment affect patient physiology and equipment.
    
- Apply this knowledge to planning safe aeromedical retrievals.
    
- Integrate concepts across assessments.
