**• Otoscope technique** 
o Examine “better” / non-painful side first 
o Ensure light is working on otoscope 
o Otoscope should be held in same hand as ear being tested, held like a pencil with hand resting on patients’ cheek to prevent damage from sudden movements 
o Pull pinna upwards and backwards with other hand to straighten external auditory canal 
o Position otoscope at external auditory meatus o Advance otoscope under direct vision, be gentle and slow with movements

**• External auditory canal assessment** 
o Excessive ear wax – most common cause of hearing loss 
o Erythema and oedema – typical with otitis externa 
o Discharge – may suggest otitis externa or otitis media with associated tympanic membrane perforation 
o Foreign bodies – cotton buds, insects and other small objects

**• Tympanic membrane assessment** 
o Colour – should appear pearly grey and translucent / erythema may indicate otitis media 
o Shape – should appear relatively flat / bulging may suggest increased middle ear pressure (common with otitis media) / retraction suggests reduced middle ear pressure (may indicate upper respiratory tract infections and allergies) 
o Perforation – note size and position of perforation (causes may include infection, trauma, cholesteatoma, grommets) o Scarring (tympanosclerosis) – can cause significant conductive hearing loss
