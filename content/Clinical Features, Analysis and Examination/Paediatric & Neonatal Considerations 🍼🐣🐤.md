The “80 mL of blood per kilogram” rule of thumb is most accurate for infants and young children up to about 6 years old. As children grow, their body composition changes, and the blood volume per kilogram gradually decreases toward adult values.

Here’s a rough guide for blood volume by age:

Age Group	Blood Volume Estimate

Neonates (0–1 month)	~85–90 mL/kg
Infants (1–12 months)	~75–80 mL/kg
Children (1–6 years)	~75–80 mL/kg
Older children (7–12)	~70–75 mL/kg
Adolescents & adults	~65–70 mL/kg


So, when does it shift?

Around 6–7 years old, the child’s blood volume per kg starts trending downward from 80 mL/kg to adult values (~70 mL/kg).

By 12 years old or during puberty, most children have reached adult blood volume norms per kg.


Summary:

The 80 mL/kg rule is most relevant from infancy to around 6 years old, and becomes less accurate as the child approaches adolescence.
