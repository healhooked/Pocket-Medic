Variables of Thrombus Formation

**Virchow's triad** is a concept that describes three primary factors contributing to the development of thrombosis. These factors are:

1. **Endothelial Injury**: Damage to the endothelial lining of blood vessels can initiate thrombus formation. This can be due to physical trauma, inflammation, or other pathological conditions affecting the vessel wall.[1-2]

2. **Hypercoagulability**: This refers to an increased tendency of the blood to clot. It can be due to genetic factors (e.g., Factor V Leiden mutation), acquired conditions (e.g., antiphospholipid syndrome), or other states that alter the balance of procoagulant and anticoagulant factors in the blood.[1-2]

3. **Stasis of Blood Flow**: Reduced or stagnant blood flow can lead to clot formation. This can occur in situations such as prolonged immobility, venous obstruction, or heart failure, where the normal flow of blood is disrupted.[1-2]

These three factors interact and contribute to the pathogenesis of both venous and arterial thrombosis. Understanding Virchow's triad is crucial for identifying risk factors and implementing preventive measures in clinical practice.
