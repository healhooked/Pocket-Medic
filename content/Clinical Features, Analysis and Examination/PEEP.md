In medicine, "PEEP" stands for Positive End-Expiratory Pressure. It is a mode of mechanical ventilation used to maintain a certain level of pressure in the airways at the end of expiration. This helps to keep the alveoli (the tiny air sacs in the lungs) open, improving oxygenation and preventing collapse of the lungs (atelectasis). 

PEEP can be beneficial in patients with conditions such as acute respiratory distress syndrome (ARDS) or other forms of respiratory failure, as it enhances lung compliance and gas exchange. However, it must be carefully monitored, as excessive PEEP can lead to decreased cardiac output or barotrauma.
