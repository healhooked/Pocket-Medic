During massive exsanguination, the oxygen delivery to the tissues is insufficient to meet the demand. A forced transition to anaerobic metabolism (characterized by the generation of lactic acid, inorganic phosphates and oxygen radicals) occurs, leading to injury and/or cell death. Hypoxia and derivated tissue derrangements triggered by the decrease of adenosine triphosphate (ATP), finally promote disruption of mithocondrial and cell membranes, apoptosis or necroptosis. [8](http://www.scielo.org.co/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0120-33472022000400301#B8) These events derived from hypoxia due to hypovolemia or reactive vasoconstriction led to progressive multiorgan failure. Lethal cases of brain and myocardial hypoperfusion could cause cerebral anoxia and fatal arrythmias in a matter of minutes. [8](http://www.scielo.org.co/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0120-33472022000400301#B8) In addition to multiple organ failure, during hemorrhagic shock, induced endothelial activation and damage, is expressed as distributive shock. [8](http://www.scielo.org.co/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0120-33472022000400301#B8)

The massive release of catecholamines is a catalyst of endothelial cell injury and destruction of the glycocalyx [8](http://www.scielo.org.co/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0120-33472022000400301#B8) ([Figure 2](http://www.scielo.org.co/scielo.php?script=sci_arttext&pid=S0120-33472022000400301#f2)).
