*Response:*

*Critical Hemorrhage?*

*Airway*
Assessment of the conscious patient: • Assess for foreign objects in the patient’s mouth • Ask the patient to remove objects from his mouth (e.g. food, gum, broken teeth, or vomit) Assessment of the unconscious patient: • Look, listen, and feel for breath sounds to indicate if the airway is patent • Assess for potential blockages Treatments: STOP AND FIX any airway compromises. Interventions may include the head-tilt-chin lift, jaw thrust (see illustrations below), removal of foreign objects from the mouth, and Heimlich maneuver. If no chance of spine injury, and appears patient may vomit, position on his or her side.

*Breathing*
- Depth
- Rate
- Speech

Assessment of the conscious patient: • Place hands on the chest wall below the armpits. Ask the patient to take a deep breath. Is there any pain associated with the breath? • Listen to the quality and rate of breath sounds. Normal is 12 to 18 per minute. • Listen for sounds of wheezing, gurgling or escaping air • Expose the chest wall looking for injury or instability Assessment of the unconscious patient: • Listen for the presence (chest rise) and quality of breath sounds • Listen for sounds of gurgling or escaping air Treatments: STOP AND FIX any life-threatening breathing issue. Interventions may include rescue breaths, emergency inhalers, and stopping air leaks. Rescue breathing: Each breath should be delivered over 1 second with visible chest rise. If the breath will not go in, reposition the airway and attempt again

*Circulation*
- Radial Pulse?
- Carotid Pulse?
• Assess pulse (radial, femoral, carotid or brachial) Normal is 55 to 90 per minute. Is it regular, irregular, bounding, rapid, weak/thready? • Sweep head to toe for blood to identify life-threatening bleeding. Also note significant swelling or major bone deformities. Look on the ground for significant blood loss. Treatments: STOP AND FIX patient who is pulse-less or has life-threatening bleeding. Interventions may include well-aimed direct pressure, tourniquet or CPR.

*Disability:*
- [[GCS 1]]
- [[Pupil Analysis]]
Determine alertness (AVPU) and mental status (What is your name? What is the date? Where are you?) Determine the mechanism of injury (MOI) and make a decision regarding likelihood of spine injury. Maintain spine immobilization if a mechanism of injury to the spinal column is present. Assume positive MOI for spinal cord injury if the patient is or has experienced any of the following issues during the incident: • Found unconscious • Does not recall the events immediately preceding the injury • Fell from a height greater than his own • Exhibits signs and symptoms of a head injury • A violent change in velocity • A blunt trauma to the head, neck, or back
*Environment*
Expose the patient inspecting for any additional life threats with appropriate sensitivity and modesty Minimize environmental exposure. Place on an insulated surface. Recall that patient is more vulnerable to develop hypothermia or hyperthermia than the rescuer. Start thinking about if and how soon the patient requires evacuation. Take stock of the resources you have, determine what you will require and how you will attempt to obtain them.
