Ibogaine is a psychoactive substance derived from the root bark of the African plant Tabernanthe iboga. It is primarily known for its use in the treatment of opioid addiction and other substance use disorders.

### Mechanism of Action:
- **NMDA Receptor Antagonist**: Ibogaine acts on the NMDA receptors, which may help in reducing cravings and withdrawal symptoms.
- **Opioid Receptor Interaction**: It has effects on opioid receptors, potentially reducing the effects of opioids and alleviating withdrawal.
- **Psychoactive Effects**: The hallucinogenic properties of ibogaine may facilitate introspection and psychological healing during treatment.

### Indication:
- Ibogaine is used in some alternative treatment settings for opioid addiction and other substance use disorders, although it is not approved by regulatory agencies in many countries.

### Drug Class:
- **Psychoactive Substances** (not classified under traditional drug classes like opioids or stimulants).

### Side Effects:
- Hallucinations
- Nausea
- Ataxia (loss of coordination)
- Cardiac complications (risk of arrhythmias)
- Anxiety
- Fatigue
