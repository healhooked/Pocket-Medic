
| **Medication**                           | **Mechanism of Action**                                   | **Used For**                             | **Notes**                                          |
| ---------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| **Sodium Valproate** (Valproate, Epilim) | ↑ GABA activity, Na⁺ channel blocker                      | Generalised & focal seizures, absence    | Avoid in pregnancy (teratogenic), liver monitoring |
| **Lamotrigine**                          | Inhibits voltage-gated Na⁺ channels → ↓ glutamate release | Focal & generalised seizures             | Rash risk (Stevens-Johnson), titrate slowly        |
| **Levetiracetam** (Keppra)               | Binds SV2A → modulates neurotransmitter release           | Broad-spectrum (focal & generalised)     | Well tolerated, low interactions                   |
| **Carbamazepine**                        | Blocks voltage-gated Na⁺ channels                         | Focal seizures                           | Enzyme inducer, avoid in generalised seizures      |
| **Phenytoin**                            | Na⁺ channel blocker                                       | Status epilepticus (after benzos), focal | Narrow therapeutic index, many drug interactions   |
| **Clonazepam**                           | Enhances GABA-A receptor activity (benzo)                 | Myoclonic seizures, acute rescue         | Sedating, tolerance develops                       |
| **Diazepam / Midazolam**                 | Benzodiazepines: enhance GABA-A activity                  | Acute seizures, status epilepticus       | First-line emergency use                           |
| **Topiramate**                           | Blocks Na⁺ channels, enhances GABA, ↓ glutamate           | Generalised & focal seizures             | Cognitive side effects, weight loss                |
| **Ethosuximide**                         | Blocks T-type Ca²⁺ channels                               | Absence seizures                         | Specific for absence seizures                      |
| **Phenobarbital**                        | Enhances GABA-A receptor activity                         | Refractory seizures, neonatal seizures   | Sedating, long half-life                           |
### Reference Guide

- **First-line in emergencies**: Midazolam (IM/buccal), Diazepam (IV/rectal).
- **Broad-spectrum**: Valproate, Lamotrigine, Levetiracetam.
- **Narrow-spectrum (focal)**: Carbamazepine, Phenytoin.
- **Absence-only**: Ethosuximide.
- **Pregnancy caution**: Avoid Valproate; Lamotrigine preferred if needed.
- **Mechanism groups**:
    - 🧠 **Na⁺ channel blockers**: Lamotrigine, Phenytoin, Carbamazepine
    - 🛑 **GABA enhancers**: Benzos, Phenobarbital, Valproate
    - ⚡ **Others**: Levetiracetam (SV2A), Ethosuximide (Ca²⁺)
