Notes from Lectures:
**Definition:**
OD on TCAs such as Amitriptyline:  
•>10 mg/kg is potentially life‐threatening.  
•>30 mg/kg is expected to result in severe toxicity with pH‐dependent cardiotoxicity and coma lasting >24 hours.  
•Clomipramine  
•Imipramine  

**Main Risks:**
•CNS – seizures  
•CVS – hypotension  
•Anticholinergic – tachy, fever, dry mouth, AMS  
•Cardiac – fast Na channel blockade  

**Mechanism:**
Electrophysiological effects:  
- Na channel blockade => slows the action potential in myocardial cells.  
- The characteristic QRS prolongation seen in TCA overdose occurs secondary to prolongation of the myocardial action potential. 
- This effect can lead to heart block and bradycardia.  
- K channel blockade causes QT prolongation => causing torsades de pointes (TdP).  
- TCAs can also exert a quinidine‐like toxic effect on the myocardium that can cause decreased cardiac contractility and hypotension.

**ECG Features:**  
• Widened QRS  
• QT prolongation => Torsades de pointes
