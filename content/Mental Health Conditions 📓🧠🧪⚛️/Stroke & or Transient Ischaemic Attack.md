# [[Cranial Nerves, Examination]]
Core high-yield cranial nerve tests (stroke/TIA)

CN II – Optic (vision)
Visual acuity (gross: can they read or count fingers)
Visual fields (confrontation)
Look for homonymous hemianopia → strongly suggests cortical stroke (often MCA/PCA territory)

CN III, IV, VI – Eye movements
“Follow my finger” (H-pattern)
Check for:
Diplopia
Gaze palsy (can’t look one direction → brainstem stroke)
Nystagmus (central vs peripheral differentiation)
👉 Key stroke sign: conjugate gaze deviation (eyes “stuck” toward one side)

CN V – Trigeminal
Light touch to face (compare sides)
Jaw clench (motor)
👉 Less sensitive for stroke alone, but useful for brainstem involvement

CN VII – Facial nerve (VERY high yield)
Ask patient to:
Smile
Show teeth
Raise eyebrows
👉 Central (UMN) lesion:
Lower face weakness only (forehead spared)
👉 Peripheral lesion (e.g., Bell’s palsy):
Entire half of face weak
This distinction is critical in stroke vs mimic

CN VIII – Vestibulocochlear
Hearing grossly
More importantly: vestibular function
If patient has vertigo:
Perform HINTS exam (only if trained):
Head impulse
Nystagmus
Test of skew
👉 Central findings = think posterior circulation stroke

CN IX, X – Glossopharyngeal/Vagus
Voice quality (hoarse? dysarthric?)
Swallow (ask about choking)
Say “ah” → uvula deviation
👉 Red flags:
Dysphagia
Dysarthria
“Wet voice” → aspiration risk

CN XI – Accessory
Shoulder shrug (weakness = lateral brainstem involvement)

CN XII – Hypoglossal
Stick out tongue
👉 Deviates away from cortical lesion
👉 Dysarthria is often more clinically useful than deviation alone

# What actually matters clinically (don’t overcomplicate)
In real stroke/TIA assessment, these give you the most signal:

- Facial droop (CN VII)
- Speech (CN X/XII cortical control)
- Gaze deviation (III, IV, VI)
- Visual field loss (CN II)
- Ataxia/vertigo signs (VIII, cerebellar pathways)
- This is why tools like:
- FAST (Face, Arm, Speech, Time)
- BE-FAST (adds Balance, Eyes)
- exist—they compress cranial nerve + motor findings into usable screening.
