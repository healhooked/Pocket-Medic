[[Neurodevelopmental disorder]]
- Persistent deficits in social communication and social interaction, spanning multiple contexts
1. social and emotional reciprocity
2. deficits in nonverbal communcative behavours
3. Deficits in developing, maintaining and understanding relationships

- Restricted and repetitive patterns of behaviour, interests or activities, in at least two  of the following:
1. Sterotyped or repetitive motor movements
2. Insistence on sameeness, inflexible adherence to routines
3. Highly restricted, fixated interests abnormal in intensity and focus
4. Hyper- or hypo reactivity to sensory input

Symptoms must be present in early developmental period

[[Monoamine Dysfunction]]
- mesolimbic and mesocortical, abnormal pathways in social and repetitive behaviours.

[[Pathophysiology]]:
- multifactoral
- Genetic component
- Mutations and polymorphisms e.g. CHD8, NLGN3

Neuroanatomical Changes:
- Increased brain volume
- Enlarged amygdala

**[[Communication]]

***Notes on Communicating with the ASD Patient:***


1. **Clear and Direct Language**: Use straightforward, literal language. Avoid idioms, metaphors, or ambiguous phrases that may be confusing.
2. **Visual Supports**: Many autistic individuals benefit from visual aids, such as pictures, charts, or written instructions, to help convey information.
3. **Give Time to Process**: Allow extra time for the individual to process information and respond. Avoid rushing them or interrupting.
4. **Be Patient and Understanding**: Show patience and understanding, especially if the individual struggles with verbal communication. Non-verbal communication methods, such as gestures or writing, may be more effective.
5. **Respect Personal Space**: Be mindful of personal space and sensory sensitivities. Some individuals may prefer more physical distance during conversations.
6. **Use of Routine and Structure**: Many autistic individuals thrive on routine and predictability. Providing structure in communication can help them feel more comfortable.
7. **Ask About Preferences**: If appropriate, ask the individual how they prefer to communicate. This can help tailor the interaction to their specific needs.
8. **Avoid Overstimulation**: Be aware of the environment and minimize sensory overload, such as loud noises or bright lights, which can be distracting or distressing.
9. **Focus on Interests**: Engaging in topics that interest the individual can facilitate communication and make interactions more enjoyable.
10. **Non-Verbal Communication**: Pay attention to non-verbal cues, as some autistic individuals may communicate their feelings or needs through body language or facial expressions.
