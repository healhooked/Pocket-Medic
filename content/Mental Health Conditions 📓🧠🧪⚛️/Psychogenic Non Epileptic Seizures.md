Seizure-like episodes without abnormal cortical electrical activity → psychological/functional origin (subset of Functional Neurological Disorder).

# Core mechanism
Top-down dysregulation of motor/sensory networks → real symptoms, no EEG correlate.

# Key differences vs epilepsy
EEG: normal during event
Cause: psychological, not neuronal discharge
AEDs: ineffective

# Red flags for PNES
- Long duration (>2–3 min), fluctuating course
- Asynchronous, irregular movements (not tonic–clonic pattern)
- Eyes closed (often tightly)
- Pelvic thrusting, side-to-side head movement
- Crying, vocalisation during event
- Rapid recovery, minimal post-ictal phase

# Common associations
- Trauma / PTSD
- Anxiety, depression

# Prior healthcare exposure / chronic illness
Prehospital priorities (practical)
Airway, breathing, circulation → always first
Check BGL, rule out organic causes
Avoid escalating to heavy sedation unless clear epileptic status
Reassurance, low-stimulation environment
High-risk pitfall
Misdiagnosed PNES → unnecessary benzos → intubation cascade
