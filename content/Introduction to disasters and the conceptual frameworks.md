**Main Categories of Disasters:**

1. **Natural Disasters**

- **Geophysical** (Earthquakes, Volcanoes, Landslides)
- **Hydrological** (Floods, Tsunamis)
- **Meteorological** (Cyclones, Storms)
- **Climatological** (Droughts, Wildfires)
- **Biological** (Pandemics, Invasive Species)

1. **Man-Made (Anthropogenic) Disasters**

- **Technological** (Industrial Accidents, Oil Spills)
- **Environmental** (Deforestation, Pollution)
- **Societal** (Terrorism, War, Cyber Attacks)

##### **Disaster Management Cycle**

The Disaster Management Cycle involves four types of activities that contribute to the reduction or elimination of hazards and reducing the susceptibility/increasing the resilience of the community to the impact of those hazards.

Image source:  Putrananda, A. What is a disaster? Quora https://www.quora.com/What-is-disaster-management-cycle

**Mitigation (sometimes called Prevention):** Involves directly preventing future emergencies and/or minimizing their negative effects.  For example:

- Public education
- Hazard and vulnerability assessment
- Improved infrastructure i.e. building back better

At an individual level, buying flood and fire insurance for your home is a mitigation activity. Mitigation activities take place **before and after** emergencies.

 **Preparedness:** Include plans or preparations made in advance of an emergency that help individuals and communities get ready. For example:

- Emergency response plans
- Training& Exercises
- Sirens

At an individual level stocking food and water before a cyclone is a preparedness activity. Preparedness activities take place **before** an emergency occurs.

**Response:** Disaster response work includes any actions taken in the midst of or immediately following an emergency, including efforts to save lives and to prevent further property damage. For example:

- Incident stabilization
- Property preservation
- Evacuation& Shelters
- Mass care

At an individual level, seeking shelter from a cyclone or turning off gas valves in an earthquake are both response activities. Response activities take place **during** an emergency.

**Recovery:** Recovery happens after damages have been assessed, and involves actions to return the affected community to its pre-disaster state or better – and ideally to make it less vulnerable to future risk. For example:

- Economic recovery
- Debris management
- Housing
- Health and social services

 At an individual level, recovery includes getting financial assistance to help pay for the repairs. Recovery activities take place **after** an emergency.

###### 🛠️ Activity

Go to your local council website.

- Is there a section on community safety/disasters/emergencies?
- What resources are available for the general community?
- What information is available about the local disaster management group?
- Is a local disaster management plan available?

NB If your local council has very little available, look at a council area that has experienced natural disasters in the past, i.e. bushfire or cyclone-prone regions, as a comparison.
