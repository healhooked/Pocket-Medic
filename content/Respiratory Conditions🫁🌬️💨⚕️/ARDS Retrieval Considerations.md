# **1. ARDS Pathophysiology (Quick Recall: D.A.M.P.P.)**

ARDS = **D**iffuse **A**lveolar **M**embrane **P**ermeability ↑ → **P**oor oxygenation

Key features:

- **Alveolar flooding** (protein‑rich fluid)
- **Loss of surfactant** → alveolar collapse
- **Shunt physiology** → poor response to O₂
- **Low compliance** → high ventilatory pressures required
- **High dead space** → ↑ work of breathing
- **Prone to barotrauma**, especially at altitude

---

# **2. Why ARDS patients deteriorate at altitude**

ARDS + altitude physiology = **dangerous combination**  
Key issues:

### **(A) Dalton’s Law → Less available O₂ → Hypoxia worsens**

ARDS already has:

- Shunt physiology
- Ventilation–perfusion mismatch
- Low PaO₂ despite FiO₂

Even small altitude pressure drops cause **major desaturation**.

### **(B) Boyle’s Law → Barotrauma risk ↑**

ARDS patients have:

- Stiff lungs
- High airway pressures
- Bullae or alveolar fragility

Altitude expansion can precipitate:

- Pneumothorax
- Pneumomediastinum
- Tension physiology

### **(C) Cabin humidity & temp → Airway secretions thicken**

Dry air + cold temps → difficult ventilation and increased ETT obstruction risk.

### **(D) Vibration & limited space → Ventilation challenges**

- Hard to titrate pressures
- Deterioration harder to detect
- Prone to derecruitment with movement/transfer

---

# **3. Clinical Management for ARDS in Retrieval (High Yield)**

## **A. Oxygenation Strategy**

ARDS requires **lung‑protective ventilation**:

### **🔹 Low tidal volume ventilation**

6 mL/kg ideal body weight

### **🔹 High PEEP strategy (recruitment dependent)**

Typical:

- PEEP 10–15 cmH₂O (or higher if ICU‑established)

### **🔹 FiO₂ 1.0 initially in retrieval**

Altitude = ↓ PaO₂ → always pre‑oxygenate

### **🔹 Ensure permissive hypercapnia is acceptable**

Goal: pH > 7.20 (unless severe TBI)

---

## **B. Ventilator Settings (Transport‑Optimised)**

### **Use: Pressure‑controlled or volume‑controlled lung protective mode**

Recommended:

- TV 4–6 mL/kg
- PEEP 10–15+
- Plateau pressure < 30 cmH₂O
- RR adjusted to maintain pH > 7.20
- FiO₂ at least 0.6, often 1.0 for takeoff/landing

### **Avoid derecruitment**

- Minimise disconnections
- Use closed suction system
- Pre‑oxygenate before any circuit break

---

## **C. Risk of Pneumothorax (Critical)**

ARDS + altitude = **high barotrauma risk**, sometimes silent.

Checklist:

- Ensure bilateral chest movement symmetrical
- Have thoracostomy kit immediately ready
- Consider prophylactic venting if high suspicion (ICU‑dependent decision)

---

## **D. Positioning**

If tolerated:

- **Head‑up 20–30°** for oxygenation + secretion drainage
- True prone ventilation is not feasible in standard aircraft
    - Some fixed‑wing ICU jets _can_, but rarely used

---

## **E. Haemodynamic Management**

ARDS patients often have:

- Relative hypovolaemia (due to PEEP)
- Vasoplegia (sepsis-related)
- RV strain (high PEEP + hypoxia ↓ RV output)

**Management:**

- Consider low‑dose vasopressors (norepinephrine) to maintain MAP ≥ 65
- Avoid over-resuscitation → pulmonary oedema worsens
- Monitor for acute cor pulmonale (drop in BP after PEEP ↑)

---

## **F. Sedation & Paralysis**

Often required to:

- Prevent ventilator dyssynchrony
- Maintain lung-protective ventilation
- Avoid elevations in oxygen demand

Useful agents:

- Fentanyl + Ketamine
- Propofol (if haemodynamics tolerate)
- Rocuronium for paralysis if needed

---

# **4. Transport Platform Decision**

### **🚁 Rotary Wing: High Risk**

Why?

- Unpressurised cabin → altitude hypoxia
- Vibration → derecruitment
- Limited space → hard to manage ARDS safely

→ **Avoid helicopter transport for ARDS unless absolutely unavoidable**  
→ If used, fly at **lowest altitude possible**

### ✈️ **Fixed Wing (Pressurised)** = Preferred

- Cabin altitude can be set to sea-level pressure
- More room for ventilator management
- Better environmental control

---

# **5. Pre‑Flight Checklist for ARDS Patients (High‑Yield)**

- Secure ETT (ARDS patients often difficult to reintubate)
- Confirm CXR if possible (exclude pneumo)
- Inline suction connected
- Vent settings rehearsed; PEEP stabilised before departure
- Ensure sedation & paralysis adequate
- IV access x2 + vasoactive infusions ready
- Check battery life on ventilator + backup O₂
- Consider arterial line for ICU‑level retrievals
- Carry spare ventilator tubing & filters
- Plan lowest cabin altitude possible

---

# **6. Key Red Flags for Deterioration In Flight**

- Sudden desaturation
- Rising airway pressures
- Hypotension (suggesting tension pneumothorax or RV failure)
- Asynchrony with ventilation
- CO₂ retention with falling pH

**Immediate actions:**

- 100% O₂
- Disconnect and gently bag with PEEP valve
- Needle decompression if tension suspected
- Reassess airway, ETT, circuit
- Adjust PEEP and pressures
