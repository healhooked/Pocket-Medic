> An ST segment is considered elevated when it is **1 mm (0.1 mV) above the baseline, measured 0.04 second (1 small square) after the J point** of the QRS complex [8]. ST elevation usually appears within minutes after the onset of infarction [14].

National Heart Foundation of Australia and the Cardiac Society of Australia and New Zealand.
• **ST-segment elevation in ≥ 2 contiguous leads**:
• **≥ 2.5 mm** in leads V2-V3 in men under 40 years.
• **≥ 2.0 mm** in leads V2-V3 in men over 40 years.
• **≥ 1.5 mm** in leads V2-V3 in women.
• **≥ 1.0 mm** in other leads.[1-2]
• **Development of new onset left bundle-branch block (LBBB)**.[1-2]

The criteria you provided specify "2 mm or more ST elevation in the precordial leads," which is accurate for men over 40 years but not for men under 40 years or women. Additionally, the mention of "1 mm or more reciprocal changes" is not a standard criterion for diagnosing STEMI but can support the diagnosis.

Therefore, the criteria should be adjusted to reflect the specific thresholds for different populations as outlined by the National Heart Foundation of Australia and the Cardiac Society of Australia and New Zealand.[1-2]
