Mifepristone is a competitive progesterone receptor antagonist that disrupts pregnancy through multiple synergistic mechanisms affecting the endometrium, myometrium, cervix, and placenta.[1][2]

Molecular Mechanism

Mifepristone binds to progesterone receptors with five times greater affinity than progesterone itself.[3] Unlike progesterone, the mifepristone-receptor complex inhibits transcription and down-regulates progesterone-dependent genes.[3] This competitive antagonism blocks the normal progesterone-mediated effects that maintain pregnancy.[2]

Effects on the Endometrium and Placenta

By blocking progesterone receptors, mifepristone causes:

- Decidual necrosis and detachment of the blastocyst from the uterine wall[2][3]
- Disruption of placental function, including direct inhibition of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG), human placental lactogen, and progesterone production by the trophoblast[4]
- Damage to endometrial blood vessels, further compromising embryo viability[3]

The withdrawal of progesterone support leads to menstrual bleeding and shedding of pregnancy tissue.[5]

Effects on the Myometrium

Mifepristone has three distinct effects on uterine contractility:[5]

1. Increases prostaglandin synthesis: Stimulates decidual cells to secrete prostaglandin F2α
2. Decreases prostaglandin metabolism: Reduces breakdown of endogenous prostaglandins
3. Sensitizes the myometrium to prostaglandins: Enhances myometrial response to both endogenous and exogenous prostaglandins, promoting coordinated contractions with increased amplitude and frequency[1][2][5]

Notably, mifepristone increases uterine contractility even when prostaglandin synthesis is blocked by indomethacin, indicating additional prostaglandin-independent mechanisms.[5]

Effects on the Cervix

Mifepristone causes marked cervical softening and dilation.[5] This effect is independent of local prostaglandin release and occurs through direct progesterone receptor antagonism.[5] The cervical ripening facilitates expulsion of pregnancy tissue and is why mifepristone is also used for cervical preparation before surgical procedures.[5]

Clinical Significance

These combined effects explain why mifepristone is particularly useful in missed miscarriage, where a non-viable pregnancy is retained in the uterus with a closed cervical os.[6] The anti-progesterone effect initiates tissue shedding, while cervical softening and myometrial sensitization prepare the uterus for subsequent misoprostol administration.[6][1] In contrast, mifepristone is considered less essential in incomplete miscarriage, where the process has already begun and treatment primarily aims to stimulate uterine contractility.[6]
