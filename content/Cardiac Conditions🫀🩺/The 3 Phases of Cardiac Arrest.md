 Time-sensitive three phases of cardiac arrest with survival rates. CPR: Cardiopulmonary resuscitation.

Good Quality Perfusion and Ventilation during Circulatory & Metabolic phases is required for reuptake of O2 in substrate to be more suscepitable to ROSC following defibrillation

1. Electrical •Duration: 0‐4 minutes post‐arrest •Characteristics: The heart is typically in ventricular fibrillation (VF) or ventricular tachycardia (VT). • Intervention: Immediate defibrillation is most effective. • Evidence: Studies show a high successrate of defibrillation if performed within the first few minutes(Zipes et al., Volosin et al.). 
2. Circulatory •Duration: 4‐10 minutes post‐arrest •Characteristics:Oxygen stores are depleted, and the body shifts to anaerobic metabolism. • Intervention: Chest compressions and ventilation to maintain myocardial perfusion and oxygenation. • Evidence: Compressions before defibrillation significantly improve outcomes(Yakaitis et al., Niemann et al.). 3. 
3. Metabolic •Duration: Beyond 10 minutes post‐arrest •Characteristics: Severe hypoxia and acidosisset in, leading to extensive cellular damage and decreased chances of successfulresuscitation. • Intervention: Advanced life support measures, including medications and potentially mechanical interventions. •Challenges: Reperfusion injury and severe electrolyte imbalances complicate resuscitation efforts.
