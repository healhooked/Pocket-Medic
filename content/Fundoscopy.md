**Ophthalmoscope settings** 
o If you and the patient have normal visual acuity, set the dial to 0 (e.g. 0 + 0 = 0). 
o If you have a refractive error but are planning to wear glasses/contact lenses that correct this, assume you have a refractive error of 0 and add the patient’s refractive error to this (e.g. 0 + -2 = -2). o If the patient has a refractive error and you have normal visual acuity set the dial to the net refractive error. An example of this would be (your refractive error of 0) + (patient’s refractive error of -2) = a setting of -2. 
o If the patient has a refractive error and you have a refractive error set the dial to the net refractive error. An example of this would be (your refractive error of +3) + (patient’s refractive error of -2) = a setting of +1. o If things appear out of focus during the assessment, simply adjust the diopter dial until things look sharper. 


o Hold ophthalmoscope in same hand as eye being tested, place other hand on patient’s forehead to avoid collision 
o Approach from 10-15° angle slightly temporal to the patient, move closer whilst maintaining fundal reflex (reddish-orange reflection of light from back of the eye)

**• Assess the optic disk** 
o Identify a blood vessel and follow the branches to the optic disk 
o Contour – borders should be clear and well defined, blurred borders may suggest optic disk swelling (secondary to raised intracranial pressure) 
o Colour – should look like an orange-pink donut, pale optic disk suggests presence of optic atrophy (optic neuritis, advanced glaucoma, ischaemic vascular event)
o Cup (pale centre of the orange-pink optic disk) – pale cup colour suggests absence neuro-retinal tissue. Cup should be on third the height of the optic disk, enlarged cup suggests reduced volume health neuro-retinal tissue (can occur in glaucoma)

**• Assess the retina** 
o Assess each quadrant – superior temporal / superior nasal / inferior nasal / inferior temporal 
o Arteriolar narrowing – copper or silver wire appearance (early stages hypertensive retinopathy) 
o Dot and blot haemorrhages – bleeding capillaries look like microaneurysms (diabetic retinopathy) o Flame haemorrhages – larger haemorrhages with flame-like appearance (grade 3 hypertensive retinopathy, thrombocytopaenia, vein occlusion, trauma) 
o Cotton wool spots – small, fluffy, whitish superficial lesions (diabetic retinopathy, grade 3 hypertensive retinopathy)

**• Assess the macula** 
o Ask patient to briefly look directly into the light of the ophthalmoscope 
o Macula is lateral (temporal) to the optic nerve head and is yellow in colour 
o The “fovea”, the central part of the macula is similar diameter as the optic disc, appears darker than rest of the macula due to additional pigment 
o Hard exudates – waxy yellow lesions, arrange in clumps or ring (diabetic retinopathy, grade 3 hypertensives retinopathy, retinal vein occlusions) 
o Drusen – yellow-white flecks scattered around macula region (age-related macular degeneration)
