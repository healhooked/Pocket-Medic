# 🔎 MODULE 1 – CONCEPTUAL FRAMEWORK OF DISASTER HEALTH

---

## 1️⃣ What Is a Disaster?

A disaster is:

> A serious disruption of the functioning of a community or society causing widespread human, material, economic or environmental losses that exceed the ability of the affected community to cope using its own resources.

Key idea:  
A disaster is **not just a hazard** — it is when hazard + vulnerability overwhelm capacity.

---

## 2️⃣ Main Categories of Disasters

### A. Natural Disasters

- **Geophysical** – Earthquakes, volcanoes, landslides
    
- **Hydrological** – Floods, tsunamis
    
- **Meteorological** – Cyclones, storms
    
- **Climatological** – Droughts, wildfires
    
- **Biological** – Pandemics, invasive species
    

### B. Man-Made (Anthropogenic) Disasters

- **Technological** – Industrial accidents, oil spills
    
- **Environmental** – Deforestation, pollution
    
- **Societal** – War, terrorism, cyberattacks
    

⚠️ Modern disasters are often **multi-hazard and cascading** (e.g., earthquake → tsunami → nuclear meltdown).

---

## 3️⃣ Disaster Management Cycle (CRITICAL FOR MCQs)

|Phase|When It Happens|Focus|Example|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Mitigation**|Before & after|Risk reduction|Building earthquake-resistant hospitals|
|**Preparedness**|Before|Readiness|Emergency plans, drills|
|**Response**|During/immediately after|Save lives|EMS, shelters|
|**Recovery**|After|Restore & improve|Rebuilding infrastructure|

### Key Distinction:

- **Mitigation = Reduce risk**
    
- **Preparedness = Get ready to respond**
    
- **Response = Immediate action**
    
- **Recovery = Restore and rebuild better**
    

⚠️ Common Quiz Trap:  
Stockpiling supplies = **Preparedness**, NOT mitigation.

---

## 4️⃣ Key Elements of Disaster Health

- Emergency Medical Services (EMS)
    
- Mental health support
    
- Infectious disease control
    
- Public health infrastructure
    
- Nutrition & food security
    

---

## 5️⃣ Disasters & Public Health Interconnection

Common impacts:

- Trauma & injuries
    
- Infectious disease outbreaks
    
- Psychological disorders (PTSD, anxiety)
    
- Malnutrition
    
- Health system collapse
    

👉 Disasters are public health crises.

# 🔎 MODULE 2 – THEORETICAL FOUNDATIONS

---

## 6️⃣ Disaster Risk Formula (EXTREMELY TESTABLE)

> **Risk = Hazard × Exposure × Vulnerability ÷ Capacity**

### Definitions:

- **Hazard** = Potential damaging event
    
- **Exposure** = People/assets in harm’s way
    
- **Vulnerability** = Susceptibility to harm
    
- **Capacity** = Ability to cope and recover
    

⚠️ Important Concept:  
If capacity increases → risk decreases.

---

## 7️⃣ Hyogo vs Sendai Framework

|Hyogo (2005–2015)|Sendai (2015–2030)|
|---|---|
|First global DRR plan|Successor framework|
|Focused on resilience|Broader multi-hazard approach|
|Risk reduction focus|Stronger governance & data emphasis|

Sendai Priorities:

1. Understand risk
    
2. Strengthen governance
    
3. Invest in resilience
    
4. Enhance preparedness & Build Back Better
    

---

## 8️⃣ Resilience (VERY TESTABLE)

> Ability to absorb shock, adapt, transform, and thrive.

Three capacities:

1. **Absorptive** – Withstand impact (e.g., seawalls)
    
2. **Adaptive** – Adjust to new conditions
    
3. **Transformative** – Structural long-term change
    

⚠️ Transformation = systemic change, not just recovery.

---

## 9️⃣ Systems Thinking

Holistic view of how parts interact.

Key features:

- Interdependencies
    
- Feedback loops
    
- Whole-system focus
    

Example:  
Health system collapse affects economy → affects recovery → increases vulnerability.

---

## 🔟 Behavioral Theories (MCQ Favourite)

These explain **why people do or do not prepare.**

- **Social Cognitive Theory** – Normalcy bias
    
- **Health Belief Model** – Perceived threat & benefit
    
- **Theory of Planned Behavior** – Attitudes + norms + control
    
- **Locus of Control** – Internal vs external control belief
    
- **Extended Parallel Process Model** – Fear + efficacy determines response
    

⚠️ Common Trap:  
Risk perception does NOT always equal preparedness.

# 🔎 MODULE 3 – MITIGATION & PREPAREDNESS

---

## 11️⃣ Mitigation vs Preparedness (CORE CONCEPT)

|Mitigation|Preparedness|
|---|---|
|Risk reduction|Readiness|
|Long-term|Operational planning|
|Prevention focus|Response focus|
|Infrastructure|Training & stockpiling|

They work together.

---

## 12️⃣ Risk Assessment

> Systematic process to identify, analyse and evaluate hazards and vulnerabilities.

### Why Important?

- Guides policy
    
- Saves lives
    
- Improves resource allocation
    
- Strengthens preparedness
    

---

## 13️⃣ INFORM Risk Index

Global tool measuring:

- Hazard exposure
    
- Vulnerability
    
- Lack of coping capacity
    

Used for:

- Resource allocation
    
- Crisis prioritisation
    
- Evidence-based planning
    

---

## 14️⃣ Haddon’s Matrix (Prevention Model)

Looks at:

- Pre-event
    
- Event
    
- Post-event
    

Across:

- Human factors
    
- Physical environment
    
- Social environment
    

Used for prevention & containment.

---

## 15️⃣ Legal & Policy Frameworks

- **Sendai Framework**
    
- **International Health Regulations (IHR 2005)**
    
- **Global Health Security Agenda (GHSA)**
    
- National Disaster Frameworks
    

These strengthen governance and coordination.

---

## 16️⃣ Barriers to Preparedness & Mitigation

- Limited resources
    
- Weak political will
    
- Misinformation
    
- Poor coordination
    
- Climate change
    
- Competing priorities

# 📍 CASE STUDY: TOHOKU EARTHQUAKE (2011)

### What Worked:

- Early warning systems
    
- Earthquake-resistant buildings
    
- Automated alerts
    
- Evacuation centres
    

### What Failed:

- Tsunami exceeded seawall design
    
- Delayed evacuations
    
- Fukushima nuclear cascading disaster
    

### Lessons:

- Plan for worst-case scenarios
    
- Prepare for cascading disasters
    
- Combine high-tech & community engagement
    
- Continuous improvement required

# 🧠 MASTER CONCEPT

Disasters occur when:

> Hazard interacts with exposed and vulnerable populations that lack sufficient capacity.

Reducing disaster risk requires:

- Understanding risk
    
- Strengthening capacity
    
- Investing in resilience
    
- Engaging communities
    
- Coordinated governance
    
- Continuous learning
