#### **Clinical Presentation**
- **Early signs**:
    - [[Hypotension]]
    - [[Tachycardia]]
    - [[Fever]]
    - Leukocytosis
- **Progression to severe/septic shock**:
-[[Perfusion Status Assessment (PSA)]]
    - Cool, clammy skin
    - Cyanosis
    - Organ dysfunction:
        - Oliguria / Acute Kidney Injury (AKI)
        - Altered mental status - *[[GCS]] ?*

> ⚠️ _Non-specific; can mimic conditions like pancreatitis or ARDS_

---
#### **Risk Factors**

- ICU admission
- Hospital acquired infection
- Bacteremia
- Advanced age
- [[Immunosuppression]]
- Recent hospitalization (especially with infection)
- Community-acquired pneumonia
- Genetic predisposition to infection

---
#### **Diagnosis**
- Based on **clinical presentation** + combination of:
    - blood tests
    - Imaging
    - Physiologic signs
    - Microbiology (cultures often negative due to prior antibiotics)
- Often made **empirically at bedside**, or retrospectively after response to antibiotics

---

#### **Prognosis**

- Mortality: **10–52%**, increasing with severity
- Elevated **post-discharge risks**:
    - Death
    - Recurrent sepsis
    - Rehospitalization
- **Poor prognostic indicators**:
    - No fever
    - Leukopenia
    - Age >40
    - Comorbidities: AIDS, cirrhosis, hepatic failure, cancer, alcohol dependence
    - Non-urinary or nosocomial source
    - Inappropriate or delayed antibiotics

***Defining Sepsis***
Sepsis exists on a continuum of severity ranging from infection (invasion of sterile tissue by organisms) and bacteremia (bacteria in the blood) to sepsis and septic shock, which can lead to multiple organ dysfunction syndrome (MODS) and death. A 2016 task force from the Society of Critical Care Medicine and European Society of Intensive Care Medicine (SCCM/ESICM) define sepsis and septic shock as the following (see 'Definitions' above):

Sepsis – Sepsis is defined as life-threatening organ dysfunction caused by a dysregulated host response to infection; organ dysfunction is defined as an increase of two or more points in the sequential (sepsis-related) organ failure assessment (SOFA) score (calculator 3).

•Septic shock – Septic shock is defined as sepsis that has circulatory, cellular, and metabolic abnormalities that are associated with a greater risk of mortality than sepsis alone; these abnormalities can be clinically identified as all of the following:

-Patients who fulfill the criteria for sepsis (above)

-Patients who, despite adequate fluid resuscitation, require vasopressors to maintain a [[Mean Arterial Pressure (MAP)]] ≥65 mmHg

-Patients who have a lactate >2 mmol/L (>18 mg/dL)
