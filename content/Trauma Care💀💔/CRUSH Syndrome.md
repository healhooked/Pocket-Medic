> [!note] Treatment
> Field
> - full monitoring
> - IV N/S 1L/hr prior to extrication
> - post extrication: 500mL N/S alternating with 500mL 5% glucose (per hour)
- prolonged ischaemic and muscular damage -> rhabdomyolysis and reperfusion injury on release
- limb often initially numb with a peripheral pulse -> rapid swelling with compartment syndrome


**COMPLICATIONS**

- hypovolaemia
- hyperkalaemia
- hypocalcaemia
- metabolic acidosis
- acute myoglobinuric renal failure
- acute muscle-crush compartment syndrome
- death
**Rationale:**

**ationale:**  
Early, aggressive fluid resuscitation before releasing the compressive force helps prevent the **massive release of potassium, myoglobin, and acids** into systemic circulation, which can cause **fatal arrhythmias**, **acute kidney injury**, and **shock**.

- **N/S:** Maintains perfusion and dilutes nephrotoxins (e.g., myoglobin).
    
- **5% Glucose:** Helps prevent hypoglycemia and supports renal perfusion. If insulin is used to treat hyperkalemia, glucose prevents hypoglycemia.

**Additional Interventions (often included):**

- **Calcium gluconate** (for cardioprotection in hyperkalemia)
    
- **Insulin + glucose**, **sodium bicarbonate**, or **nebulised salbutamol** (to shift potassium intracellularly)
    
- **Mannitol** and **urinary alkalinization (with bicarbonate)** in some settings (to prevent myoglobin-induced renal damage, though use is debated)
    
- **Avoid potassium-containing fluids** (e.g., Hartmann’s)
