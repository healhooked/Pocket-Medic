**Primary postpartum hemorrhage**:  > 500 mls bleeding **within 24 hours** of delivery. 
- Uterine atony accounts for approximately 70% of cases and is the leading etiology in this period
- Obstetric Trauma (e.g. Perineum trauma, lacerations or uterine rupture)
- Retained Placental Tissue
- Coagulation Disorders

**Secondary postpartum hemorrhage**: PV Bleeding > 500 mls, **after 24 hours and up to 12 weeks postpartum**. (1-2% of PPH)
- Subinvolution of the placental site (a failure of the fundal height to return to normal)
- Retained products of conception
- Endometritis
- Uterine vascular anomalies (such as arteriovenous malformations or pseudoaneurysms)
- Coagulopathies

**Lochia**? Normal vaginal discharge that occurs following childbirth, 
Consisting of blood, mucus, and uterine tissue. Odour?
Physiological process of uterine involution and healing of the placental site. 
- lochia rubra (red, blood-dominant, lasting several days)
- lochia serosa (pink or brown, with decreasing blood and increasing leukocytes, lasting up to two weeks), 
- lochia alba (yellow-white, predominantly leukocytes and mucus, which may persist for several weeks
#### Following Placenta being delivered:
Examine:
- **Tone** - fundus firm and central? *Continue fundal massage*, subsequent dosing and commencement of Oxytocin infusion
- **Trauma** - evidence of perineum or vaginal wall lacerations? *Control external hemorrhage ?*
- **Tissue & Thrombin** - Placenta and membranes intact? Evidence of clots? Known clotting disorder or patient is anticoagulated?
- Continue fundal massage to assist with expulsion of clots
