Placenta is situated either partially or wholly in the lower uterine segment. Relevant during third trimester 28 - 40 weeks.

This becomes relevant during the third trimester (28–40 weeks) when the downward and outward thrust of the developing foetus is accommodated by the thinning and stretching of the lower uterine wall. This expansion causes some degree of placental separation and subsequent bleeding. This can worsen during effacement of the cervix, if the placenta is near or over the cervical os (external opening of the uterus)

Antepartum haemorrhage, as initial small bleeds have the potential to develop into profuse blood loss that can threaten both the mother and the foetu

**Clinical Features**
- Several small warning bleeds 
- Bright red blood
- No pain, other than that associated with contractions
- A soft, non-tender uterus 
- Significant blood loss, which may lead to hypovolaemic shock
