***Key Details:*** Principals of Practice
- Minimise all interruptions to time on chest to ***5 seconds or less***
- Shoulder to shoulder clinician rotation for chest compressions
- Anticipatory defibrillation charging, analyse for shockable rhythm
- Nil Vomitus or secretions consider [[Laryngeal Mask Airway (LMA)]]
- Optimal [[Defibrillation Placement]]

- Ensure **high-quality CPR metrics** are maintained throughout: 
    - Early defibrillation.
    - Minimise hands off chest time or interruptions in CCs - chest compression fraction (CCF) should be > 80%.
    - Good depth and rate of compressions.
    - Effective ventilations.
    - Early drug therapy when appropriate. 
    - BLS is what saves lives, so never compromise this for ALS stuff.

### Procedure: 
- When checking the **SGA patency**, please be sure to confirm patency using the following steps: 
    - EtCO2 is the primary confirmation and will likely be around 10 mmHg or below. If not secure, there will be no EtCO2 reading. 
    - Auscultate - Ensure no air leak by listening when breaths are delivered.
    - And check for rise and fall of chest at a point when CCs are not being delivered, i.e. during rhythm analysis.
- Must maintain **defibrillation safety** by upholding these 2 x principles: 
    - Principle of "do not touch the patient whilst the monitor is charged". 
    - Principle of "no need to check for a pulse if the rhythm remains unchanged. 
- Use the **"Do/Prep"** approach below to plan ahead in the resus. 
- When performing non-stop continuous CCs after and SGA is placed, the compressor should count aloud from compression 8 to signal their partner to start reaching for the BVM to deliver a breath as this will ensure the CPG is maintained of 6 to 10 breaths delivered per minute. 

**General idea of time stamps:** 

1. Initial approach: < 1 min
    - Early CCs
    - Early pad placement with rhythm analysis
    - Place CQI equipment whilst monitor charging (Cor patch, EtCO2)
    - Early dump/shock
2. Cycle 1 - 1:00 to 3:00
    - Do: Early i-gel insertion
    - Prep: IVC
3. Cycle 2 - 3:00 to 5:00
    - Do: Complete IVC
    - Prep: Drug # 1
4. Cycle 3 - 5:00 to 7:00
    - Do: Admin Drug # 1
    - Prep: Drug # 2
5. Cycle 4 - 7:00 to 9:00
    - Do: Admin Drug # 2
    - Prep: Drug # 3
6. Cycle 5: 9:00 to 11:00
    - Do: Admin Drug # 3
    - Prep: Drug # 4

**Ventilation Strategy - During Resuscitation:** 

- High quality CPR generates only about 25 to 33% CO. 
- All ventilation strategies should be considered on V/Q matching (V = ventilation, Q = perfusion)

1. During CPR:
    - Adults: 
        - With an SGA in situ, ventilate at 6 to 10 breaths per minute (**1 breath every 6 to 10 seconds** [or every 10th compression])
        - See [QAS CPP: Resuscitation/CPRLinks to an external site.](https://www.ambulance.qld.gov.au/docs/clinical/cpp/CPP_Cardiopulmonary%20resuscitation.pdf)
    - Paediatrics
        - With an SGA in situ, ventilate at 12 to 14 breaths (**1 breath every 4 to 5 seconds** [or every 8th compression])
    - See [QAS CPP: Resuscitation/CPRLinks to an external site.](https://www.ambulance.qld.gov.au/docs/clinical/cpp/CPP_Cardiopulmonary%20resuscitation.pdf)

_____

1. Patients **with a pulse following ROSC** if hypoventilating for whatever reasons:
    - Adults: 
        - With basic or SGA in place, ventilate at 10 to 12 breaths per minute (**1 breath every 5 to 6 seconds**) to achieve the following parameters: 
            - **EtCO2 30 to 40 mmHg** (reason is to reverse acidosis from resp and metabolic causes as a result of prolonged anaerobic respiration from the arrest)
            - SpO2 > 96%
        - See - [CPG/Resuscitation: Post ROSC caresLinks to an external site.](https://www.ambulance.qld.gov.au/docs/clinical/cpg/CPG_Post%20ROSC%20management.pdf)
            - The CPG says 8 to 12 breaths per minute which works out to be 1 breath every 5 to 7.5 seconds. 
            - The modified range above is easier to remember and fits within the parameters of the CPG (who can remember 7.5 seconds?). 
    - Paediatrics: 
        - With basic or SGA in place, ventilate at 12 to 20 breaths per minute (**1 breath every 3 to 5 seconds**, possibly even quicker than this) to achieve the following parameters: 
            - **EtCO2 30 to 40 mmHg** (reason is to reverse acidosis from resp and metabolic causes as a result of prolonged anaerobic respiration from the arrest)
            - SpO2 > 96%
                - This recommendation is not found in any of the local CPGs, however is supported by international recommendations. 
                    - [AHA - Tropijan - 2020 - Part 4: Pediatric Basic and Advanced Life Support: 2020 American Heart Association Guidelines for Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation and Emergency Cardiovascular CareLinks to an external site.](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/CIR.0000000000000901)  
                        - Treatment of Respiratory Failure - Treatment of inadequate breathing with a pulse 
                            - _For the ease of training, the suggested respiratory rate for the patient with inadequate breathing and a pulse has been increased from 1 breath every 3 to 5 seconds to **1 breath every 2 to 3 seconds** to be consistent with the new CPR guideline recommendation for ventilation in patients with an advanced airway._
2. Patients with a pulse, **NOT** following ROSC, if hypoventilating for whatever reasons: 
    - Same as above, except only change is that it's a normal EtCO2 range that is being aimed for of **35 to 45 mmHg.**
