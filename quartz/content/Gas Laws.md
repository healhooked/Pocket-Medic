### **Purpose**

Understanding gas laws is critical in aeromedical retrieval to anticipate and manage physiological changes in patients and equipment during flight.
### **Boyle’s Law**

**Principle:** Volume of gas is inversely proportional to pressure (V ∝ 1/P).
- **Ascent:** Pressure ↓ → gas expands.
- **Descent:** Pressure ↑ → gas contracts.

**Clinical implications:**

- Expansion on ascent → risk of:
    - [[Tension Pneumothorax 💨🫁]]
    - Ruptured hollow viscera

- Contraction on descent → “squeeze” injuries: 
    - Barotitis media
    - Barosinusitis
    - Dental barotrauma

**Equipment implications:**
- IV tubing, ventilators, cuffed airways, air splints may expand/contract with pressure changes.
### **Dalton’s Law**
**Principle:** Total pressure of a gas mixture = sum of partial pressures (Pt = P1 + P2 + … + Pn).
- **Key point:** Oxygen is 21% of air, but **partial pressure of O₂ decreases with altitude**, causing hypoxia.
**Physiological responses to hypoxia:**
- Short-term: tachypnea, tachycardia
- Prolonged: headache, fatigue, impaired cognition → unconsciousness → death
- Individual susceptibility varies; no one is immune.

### **Charles’s Law**
**Principle:** Volume of gas ∝ temperature (V ∝ T), at constant pressure.
- Gas expands when heated, contracts when cooled.

**Clinical relevance in aeromedical retrieval:**
- Pediatric patients: small airways → more sensitive to gas volume changes
- Cooling of ventilated gases at altitude → contraction → altered airway pressures
- Equipment (ventilators, oxygen systems) must be monitored for performance changes

### **Henry’s Law**
**Principle:** Gas solubility in a liquid ∝ partial pressure of the gas (C = k·P).
- High pressure → more gas dissolves in blood
- Low pressure (ascent) → gases may come out of solution → risk of bubbles

**Clinical relevance:**
- Bubble formation can lead to decompression effects, similar to altitude sickness or gas embolism.

### **5. Key Takeaways for Retrieval Medicine**

- **Patient care:** Anticipate hypoxia, barotrauma, expansion/contraction of gases in body cavities.
- **Equipment:** Monitor for pressure and temperature effects on ventilators, IV lines, splints, endotracheal tubes.
- **Children:** More vulnerable due to smaller airways and lower functional residual capacity.
- **Planning:** Consider oxygen supplementation, cabin pressurization, and pre-flight stabilization to prevent complications.
