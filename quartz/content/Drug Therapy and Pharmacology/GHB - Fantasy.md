A patient who has taken GHB (gamma-hydroxybutyrate) can present with a wide range of symptoms, depending on the dose, time since ingestion, co-ingestants (especially alcohol), and individual physiology. GHB is a CNS depressant and acts similarly to alcohol or benzodiazepines but with a narrower therapeutic window.
---

🧠 Typical Presentation (Dose-Dependent)
🔹 Low to Moderate Dose:
[[CNS Depression]]
Euphoria
Relaxation
Disinhibition
Drowsiness
Dizziness

Nausea or vomiting
Slurred speech
Impaired coordination (ataxia)


🔹 High Dose / Overdose:

Decreased level of consciousness: can range from confusion to deep coma
Respiratory depression: slow, shallow, or irregular breathing
Bradycardia (slow heart rate)
Hypothermia
Hypotension
Myoclonic jerks or muscle twitching
Seizure-like activity (though GHB doesn’t typically cause true seizures)
Incontinence
Amnesia on waking

---

⏱️ Onset and Duration:

Onset: 10–20 minutes after ingestion

Duration: 1.5–4 hours (but coma may last longer)

Patients can have rapid onset and rapid recovery, which is a hallmark of GHB toxicity.



---

❗ Red Flags / Severe Toxicity:

Coma with preserved respiratory effort
Abrupt awakening after a period of unconsciousness (often within a few hours)
Recurrent episodes of sedation due to enterohepatic recycling
History or suspicion of use at raves, parties, or with intent for recreational or date-rape purposes



---

💡 Pearls for Clinicians:

GHB withdrawal (especially in chronic users) is severe and can be life-threatening — it resembles alcohol or benzo withdrawal but may be more intense.

Urine drug screens do not typically detect GHB.

GHB intoxication may be clinically indistinguishable from alcohol or benzo overdose, so history and scene clues are crucial.
