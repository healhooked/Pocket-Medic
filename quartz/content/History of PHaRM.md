**The History of Aeromedical Retrieval**:

---

### **Module Overview**

- Focuses on the **origins, evolution, and milestones** in aeromedical retrieval.
    
- Explores how **technological, clinical, and operational advances** shaped modern prehospital and retrieval medicine.
    
- Highlights both **Australian and international developments**.
    

### **Learning Outcomes**

- Describe the **history and evolution** of aeromedical and retrieval medicine.
    
- Appraise the **future developments** in prehospital and retrieval medicine over the next decade.
    

### **Key Historical Developments**

1. **Military Origins**
    
    - Early aeromedical transport began in **WWI (fixed-wing aircraft by French)**.
        
    - **Rotary-wing (helicopters)** first used in WWII; routine frontline evacuations became standard in **Korean War**.
        
    - **Vietnam War**: helicopters began providing **in-flight patient care**, not just transport.
        
    - These developments contributed to **improved casualty survival**, mainly through faster transfers and skilled personnel.
        
2. **Transition to Civilian Services**
    
    - **Royal Flying Doctor Service (RFDS), 1928** – model for rural medical access and longest-running aeromedical service globally.
        
    - **Helicopter use in civilian practice** began in late 1960s in Europe and post-Vietnam US.
        
    - Key Australian milestones:
        
        - Victoria’s first rescue helicopter: 1971
            
        - Westpac Life Saver Helicopter, NSW: 1973
            
        - Nationwide HEMS (CareFlight, MICA paramedics) by 1980s
            
    - US hospital-based helicopter services expanded from **1972**; by 2019, over **1,100 rotor-wing aircraft** in operation.
        
3. **Regional Contributions**
    
    - **South Australia**: early mobile intensive care models, integrating advanced clinical support with aviation operations.
        
    - Gilligan’s 1999 work documents **principles of safe transport of critically ill patients** outside hospital settings.
        
4. **Integration of Intensive Care**
    
    - Modern retrieval teams (e.g., **CCATT, E-MERT**) can transport ventilated and multi-trauma patients safely.
        
    - Civilian services now mirror military models: **multidisciplinary teams, advanced equipment**, near-hospital-level care mid-flight.
        
    - Standard crew:
        
        - **Helicopter:** doctor + paramedic
            
        - **Fixed-wing:** doctor + nurse
            
    - Routine interventions: **airway management, ventilation, blood transfusion, hemorrhage control**, plus **technical rescue capabilities**.
        

### **Future Directions**

- **Drone logistics**: AEDs, blood, medications; pre-positioned caches for disaster response.
    
- **Telemedicine**: real-time consultation, ultrasound streaming, AI-assisted monitoring.
    
- **Predictive analytics & integrated records**: bridging ICU and in-flight care.
    
- **Enhanced safety and governance**: IFR, advanced vision systems, standardized dispatch, next-gen avionics.
    

### **Reflective Activity**

- Explore the **history of local retrieval services** to understand their **roots, challenges, and contributors**.
