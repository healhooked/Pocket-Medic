## **Australian Retrieval Services (Examples)**

### **Queensland**

- **Retrieval Services Queensland (RSQ)**: Statewide clinical coordination since 2004.
    
- Helicopters: QGAir, LifeFlight, CapRescue, CQ Rescue, Babcock Australasia.
    
- Fixed-wing: RFDS (King Air B200/B350/B360), LifeFlight jets for long-haul.
    
- Paediatric retrieval: **CHQRS** (Children’s Health Queensland Retrieval Service).
    

### **South Australia**

- **SAAS MedSTAR**: Single emergency retrieval service since 2009.
    
- Covers **neonatal, paediatric, adult critical care**.
    
- Platforms: 40% helicopter, 30% fixed-wing, 30% road.
    
- Teams: Retrieval physician + nurse/paramedic; SOT paramedics on rotary wing.
    
- Central coordination through **SA Ambulance Service Emergency Operations Centre**.
    

### **New South Wales**

- Mix of government and NGO services.
    
- Central coordination via **NSW Aeromedical Operations Centre**.
    
- Specialist services: **AMRS**, **NETS** (paediatric and neonatal).
    
- Helicopters: **GSA HEMS** (flight doctor + paramedic), CareFlight, Westpac Rescue, Toll.
    

### **Western Australia**

- Mainly RFDS: 5 bases, PC12 turboprops, PC24 jets, EC145 helicopters.
    
- RAC Rescue: upgraded to AW139s; crewing moving from CCP to doctor/paramedic model.
    
- Specialist rotations and training at Jandakot (Perth) base.
    

### **Victoria**

- **Adult Retrieval Victoria (ARV)**: part of Ambulance Victoria; mixed staffing (doctors, nurses, paramedics).
    
- Paediatric/obstetric retrieval: **PIPER** across Victoria, Tasmania, and Northern NSW.
    

### **Northern Territory**

- Two systems: Top End (CareFlight NT, TEMRS), Central Australia (MRaCC + RFDS).
    
- Central coordination via **MRaCC**, staffed by specialist retrieval clinicians.

## **International Retrieval Models**

### **Anglo-American vs Franco-German**

- **Anglo-American**: “bring the patient to hospital” (load-and-go).
    
- **Franco-German**: “bring hospital to patient” (stay-and-play).
    
- Differences influenced by **training, clinical lead, system integration**.
    

### **United States**

- ~650–700 air ambulance businesses, 1,000+ helicopters, 450 fixed-wing aircraft.
    
- HEMS mainly EMS-based; physician-staffing rare.
    
- Fixed-wing: often non-emergent interfacility transfers, more likely physician-escorted.
    
- Funding: mix of **state, hospital, private enterprise, insurance**.
    

### **Germany**

- Collaboration between **ADAC, DRF, Ministry of Interior**.
    
- ~55–60 helicopter bases; staffed by **Notarzt (physician) + Rettungsassistent**.
    
- MICUs for interhospital critical transfers.
    
- Physicians typically anesthetists; Rettungsassistenten act like advanced nurses.

### **Module 1: Foundations of Prehospital and Retrieval Medicine (PHaRM)**

#### **1. Core Principles**

- Prehospital care occurs in highly variable and often unpredictable environments: e.g., cliff edges, pontoons, cattle stations, busy cities.
    
- Scene safety is paramount—for both patient and team.
    
- PHRM teams provide **specialized care** that may supplement or lead initial ambulance services.
    

#### **2. Trauma Management**

- PHRM often deals with **major trauma**: high-acuity, time-critical interventions may be required.
    
- **Key interventions include:**
    
    - Maximize tissue oxygenation.
        
    - Airway patency.
        
    - Haemorrhage control and circulatory support.
        
    - Gentle handling to preserve clot formation.
        
    - Early analgesia.
        
    - Rapid but safe transfer.
        
- **Assessment frameworks:**
    
    - **C‑ABCDE:** Catastrophic hemorrhage → Airway → Breathing → Circulation → Disability → Exposure.
        
    - **MARCH:** Massive hemorrhage → Airway → Respiration → Circulation → Hypothermia/Head injury.
        

#### **3. Examples of Prehospital Services**

- **London HEMS:** Physician-led, rapid trauma response. Attends road accidents, falls, stabbings/shootings, and major incidents.
    
- Australasian services handle both trauma and **time-critical medical emergencies** (PCI, stroke, eCPR).
    

#### **4. Interhospital Transfers (IHTs)**

- Transport between hospitals for specialized care.
    
- Challenges differ from primary scene retrieval.
    
- Requires:
    
    - Clear communication with multiple stakeholders (referring staff, pilots, family, operational crew).
        
    - Assessment if patient is **transport-critical** (needs urgent transfer) or **treatment-critical** (can benefit from in-transit care).
        
    - Good **Crew Resource Management (CRM)** and teamwork.
        

#### **5. Paediatric & Neonatal Retrievals**

- **Paediatric:**
    
    - Centralized specialized care improves outcomes.
        
    - Teams often include PICU, paediatric anesthesia, and emergency specialists.
        
    - Equipment: transport ventilators, monitors, and electronic dosing systems.
        
- **Neonatal:**
    
    - Highly specialized due to unique physiology.
        
    - Often involve **in utero transfers** when possible.
        
    - Retrieval includes stabilization before transport (“bring ICU to patient”).
        
    - Teams: medical officer/nurse practitioner + neonatal nurse; additional support if twins or complex cases.
        
    - Equipment: incubators, thermal control, drug cold chain, ventilators.
        

#### **6. Key Takeaways**

- Prehospital environments demand skill adaptation from hospital settings.
    
- **Scene safety and teamwork** are critical.
    
- PHRM teams manage trauma primarily but also handle urgent medical cases.
    
- Interhospital transfers rely on communication, stabilization, and CRM.
    
- Paediatric and neonatal retrievals require specialized teams and equipment.
    
- Coordination with wider health system improves patient outcomes.
