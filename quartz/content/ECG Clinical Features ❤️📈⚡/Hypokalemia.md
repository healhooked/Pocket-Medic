### Mechanism
Electrophysiological effects:  
• Long action potential => decrease in the difference of the resting membrane potential from  
the threshold potential during the terminal phase of the action potential and an increase in the  
relative refractory period => increases cardiac tissue excitability which can predispose to  
ectopy and PVCs for a large portion of the action potential

Decreased extracellular potassium causes myocardial hyperexcitability with the potential to develop re-entrant arrhythmias.

### ECG Features:
Increased P wave amplitude
Prolongation of PR interval
Widespread ST depression and T wave flattening/inversion
Prominent [U waves](https://litfl.com/u-wave-ecg-library/) (best seen in the precordial leads V2-V3)
_Apparent_ long QT interval due to fusion of T and U waves (= long QU interval)

**With worsening hypokalaemia…**

- Frequent supraventricular and ventricular ectopics
- Supraventricular tachyarrhythmias: AF, atrial flutter, atrial tachycardia
- [[Ventricular Tachycardia (VT)]], [[Ventricular Fibrillation (VF)]], [[Polymorphic VT]]
